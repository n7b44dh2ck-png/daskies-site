Leasing an industrial shed or warehouse in India: what to check

Renting out an empty shed brings in steady income, and renting one saves years of building. Both sides do better when these points are checked before the keys change hands.

DASKIES guide · 16 November 2026

Title, and permission to let

The person letting the property must have the right to do so. Ask for the title documents or, if the owner is itself a tenant, the head lease and the landlord's consent to sub-let.

In industrial estates run by a state corporation the land is usually held on a lease from that corporation, and its rules apply. In Gujarat, GIDC requires its permission to sub-let a plot or shed and charges a yearly sub-letting fee (listed by GIDC as 3% of the prevailing allotment price per year). GIDC does not allow sub-letting where there is unauthorised construction or use on the plot. Other states' industrial corporations have similar rules, so check before you sign.

Permitted use and approvals

  • Is the building approved for the use the tenant has in mind: manufacturing, warehousing, or both?
  • Fire safety approval for the building and the goods to be stored or processed.
  • Pollution control board consent for the tenant's activity, where it needs one. In Gujarat that is the Gujarat Pollution Control Board.

Power, water and access

  • The sanctioned electricity load, and whether it covers the tenant's machines. Increasing load takes time and money; agree who pays.
  • Water supply and effluent arrangements.
  • Truck access, loading docks, door height, floor load and clear height inside.

Put it in writing, and register longer leases

Under section 17(1)(d) of the Registration Act, 1908, read with section 107 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, a lease of immovable property from year to year, for any term longer than one year, or reserving a yearly rent must be made by a registered document, with stamp duty paid. Many short lets are written for 11 months for this reason. A longer registered lease gives both sides firmer protection.

Rent terms to agree

  • Rent, the yearly increase, and the security deposit.
  • Lock-in period and notice period for each side.
  • Who pays maintenance, property tax, insurance and the industrial estate's charges.
  • Fit-out by the tenant, and whether the space must be restored at the end.
  • What happens to machines and stock if the lease ends early.

GST on industrial rent

Renting out non-residential property, including industrial sheds and godowns, is a taxable service under GST at 18% (service code 9972). A registered landlord charges GST on the rent. If the landlord is not registered and the tenant is, the tenant may have to pay the GST under reverse charge, a rule in force since October 2024. Renting out a godown is different from providing storage services for agricultural produce, which can be exempt; your chartered accountant can tell you which applies.

Inspect before you sign

Walk the property in daylight and, if you can, after rain. Look at the roof, drainage, floor cracks, wiring, earthing and security. Photograph everything on the day the tenant takes possession and attach the photos to the lease; it saves arguments about the deposit later.
