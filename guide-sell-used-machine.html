How to sell a used industrial machine in India

An idle machine loses value every month it stands still. Selling it well takes a little preparation: clear papers, a fair value, an honest listing and the right tax treatment.

DASKIES guide · 16 November 2026

1. Gather the machine's papers

Buyers trust a machine with a history. Collect the purchase invoice, the nameplate details (make, model, serial number and year), manuals and drawings, service and repair records, and any test or calibration certificates. Note what goes with the machine: tooling, spares, attachments and software licences.

2. Make sure you can sell it

If the machine was bought on a loan or is hypothecated to a bank or NBFC, you will need the lender's no-objection or release before the sale. If the machine sits in a leased shed, check whether the lease says anything about removing plant.

3. Get a fair value

Start with what similar machines sell for, and quotes from dealers. For a figure that buyers and banks will accept, use a valuer. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India registers valuers under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, and "Plant and Machinery" is one of the three asset classes they are registered for. A valuation report also gives you a solid basis if you later swap the machine instead of selling it.

4. List it properly

  • At least three clear photos: the whole machine, a close-up of its condition, and the nameplate. A short video of it running helps more than anything else.
  • Specifications and capacity, power requirement, and current condition, stated honestly.
  • Where it is, whether it is running or dismantled, and who will handle dismantling and loading.
  • Your price, or "price on request", and whether you would rent it out or swap it.

5. Know the GST on the sale

A registered business selling a used machine charges GST on the sale at the rate that applies to that machine. There is one extra rule to check. If you claimed input tax credit when you bought the machine, section 18(6) of the CGST Act says you must pay the higher of:

  • the input tax credit you took, reduced by 5 percentage points for every quarter (or part of a quarter) since the purchase invoice; or
  • the GST on the price you are selling it for.

Example. You took ₹1,80,000 of input tax credit on a machine and sell it six quarters later. The credit is reduced by 30 percentage points, leaving ₹1,26,000. You sell the machine for ₹5,00,000; at an 18% rate, the GST on that price is ₹90,000. You pay the higher figure, ₹1,26,000. Your chartered accountant will confirm the method and rate for your machine.

6. Agree the terms in writing

  • Price, and exactly what is included.
  • Inspection: let serious buyers see it in person or on a video call before they commit.
  • Delivery terms (for example, ex-works your factory), and who pays for dismantling, loading and transport.
  • Payment schedule, such as an advance with the order and the balance before dispatch.
  • Warranty, if any. Used machines are often sold "as is, where is"; say so clearly.

7. Paperwork on the sale

  • A tax invoice to the buyer.
  • An e-way bill where the rules require one for moving the machine.
  • A delivery note signed by the buyer.
  • An update to your fixed asset register.

Selling isn't the only option

If you may need the machine again, renting it out brings in money while you keep it. If you need something else more than cash, a swap can work better than a sale. On DASKIES, buyers looking for that category see your listing, and a deal room keeps offers and terms in one place.

This guide is general information for Indian businesses, not tax or legal advice. Rules and rates change; check with your chartered accountant or lawyer before you sign or file anything.
